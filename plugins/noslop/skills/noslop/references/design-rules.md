# Design rules — Level 3

Read for content intended for visual presentation. The principles below implement the owner-approved R01–R10 decisions. The original source is archived in Git history (commit `43809893c60746bdbe95c7fadb5db812b4022c51`).

1. **Show only what helps (R02).** Prefer numbers, charts, diagrams, timelines, paths or screenshots when they *improve comprehension*. A section may be entirely prose when that is clearer. Every chosen visual must be accurately labeled and data-bearing where appropriate; no decorative charts, fake controls or placeholder metrics.
2. **Say what the reader is looking at.** Begin with concise contextual metadata (topic, source, audience, date and action where known), ideally at most 15 words. Then a truthful, specific title and one prominent ask: what is needed, from whom, by when, and how to respond, *when an ask exists*. Do not invent names, dates, approvers or reply mechanisms. Avoid duplicating the ask.
3. **Use normal sentences.** Make headings, titles and labels immediately understandable. Avoid clever, poetic or vague headlines, excessive helper text and subtitle clutter.
4. **Simple is not empty (R10).** Preserve all material facts, amounts, definitions, constraints, dates, exceptions, examples, links, owner/approver, and comparisons needed to understand or act. Every change shows an accurate baseline when one is available. If material information is unavailable, state that explicitly.
5. **Readable text, not arbitrary cutoffs (R05).** Prefer short paragraphs and short, scannable table cells. Two lines per paragraph and ten words per table cell are *guidelines*, not mandatory limits. Do not trade away meaning or accessibility to hit them. Reduce uninformative captions and unneeded grey subtext.
6. **Coherent visual system (R06).** Default to Claude's restrained design language (clay primary accent, serif headings, sans-serif body and semantic statuses). When the user requests a specific brand, theme, font or scale, honor it. Status colors communicate meaning, never decoration. See [Style kit](style-kit.md) when relevant.
7. **Clear starting point; strict structure (R03).** Make the most important fact or ask visually dominant and provide a readable hierarchy. Keep **at most four sections beneath the first screen, five entries per card/list, and one table per page/screen**. For guides and slide decks, distribute content across steps/slides as appropriate without stripping material information. If these limits cannot coexist with content integrity, request a decision rather than silently breaking either.
8. **First screen stands alone (R04).** The first screen must communicate context, central point, reasons and the action when there is one. **Require 50–120 words excluding the metadata header**, retaining the original one-page cheat-sheet/reference exception. No empty padding or fake hero height; prioritize real content.
9. **Cut peripheral information, not essential facts (R10).** Remove redundant presentation; put genuinely secondary reference information behind disclosure when appropriate to the medium. Critical reasoning, material risks, action and owner remain visible. For conflicting strict requirements, request resolution before omitting essential data.
10. **Say each thing once and check it.** Avoid repeated lists, duplicated visible figures, inconsistent labels or units, contradictory percentages and incorrect totals. A chart and its necessary accessible data representation are not pointless duplication.

These principles apply to the requested medium; they do not require HTML (R01), visualizations in every section (R02), or a particular brand when the user overrides it (R06).
